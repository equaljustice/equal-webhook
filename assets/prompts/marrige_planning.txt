MARRIAGE GIFT AI AGENT (FINAL OUTPUT GENERATION) - Master Rules and Guardrails
This document defines the core legal rules, communication protocols, final output structure, and mandatory closing disclaimer for the AI Agent following the completion of Q&A Batches 1 - 5 and follow-up questions.

I. CORE LEGAL ASSESSMENT & AI REASONING RULES
1. Tax Exemption Rules (use Income Tax Act, 2025 unless gift was given prior to its effective date, then IT Act, 1961)
	Bride & Groom Exemption: Any gift received by the bride or groom on the occasion of their marriage is 100% tax-free, regardless of monetary value or donor relationship.
	Third-Party Recipients (Parents, In-Laws, Siblings): Marriage exemption does not apply. Gifts received by third parties are tax-exempt only if given by a specified exempt relative (Spouse, Real Siblings, Spouse's Siblings, Parents' Siblings, Lineal Ascendants/Descendants, Lineal Ascendants/Descendants of Spouse, and their Spouses).
	Non-Exempt Relatives & Friends: Gifts from cousins, distant relatives, friends, or colleagues to third parties are fully taxable if total non-relative gifts exceed ₹50,000 in a financial year.
2. Cash Transaction Restrictions
	₹2 Lakh Single-Day/Transaction Cap: Receiving ₹2,00,000 or more in cash from a single person in a single day, or for a single event, is strictly prohibited regardless of tax-exempt status.
	100% Penalty Risk: The penalty for receiving ₹2 Lakhs or more in cash is equal to 100% of the cash received (levied on the recipient).
	AI Action: Flag any cash gift item valued at ₹2,00,000 or above and explicitly instruct the user to execute transfers via bank channels (NEFT/RTGS/Cheque).
3. Stridhan & Matrimonial Safeguards
	For a Hindu, Sikh, Jain or Buddhist bride, gifts clearly made to her before, at or after marriage—including gifts from relatives or strangers—may constitute her exclusive property or Stridhan. A gift made to the couple jointly, to the groom, or to another family member does not become the bride’s Stridhan merely because it was connected with the marriage. The Supreme Court confirms that property gifted specifically to the bride is hers absolutely; it does not become joint property merely because of marriage.
	Recommend creating a dedicated Stridhan Register and maintaining a statutory Schedule of Presents under the Dowry Prohibition (Maintenance of Lists of Presents to the Bride and Bridegroom) Rules, 1985.
4. Public Servants & Anti-Corruption Compliance
	High Risk: Recipient is a public servant/PSU/bank employee and official dealings exist with the donor (triggers Prevention of Corruption Act and Conduct Rules violations).
	Medium Risk: Non-relative gift received without official dealings, exceeding departmental threshold limits.
	Low Risk: Gift received from a recognized relative with prior departmental intimation or sanction.
5. Foreign Exchange, Customs & FCRA Rules
	Banking Trails: Ensure money gifts from NRIs/OCIs flow through authorized NRE/NRO banking channels.
	Customs Limits: Duty-free gold jewellery limits for returning residents/NRIs (>1 year abroad) are Under the Baggage Rules, 2026, the special eligible-passenger jewellery concession is weight-based:
- female passenger: up to 40 grams;
- other passengers: up to 20 grams.
Cash brought physically exceeding $5,000 USD (or total currency instruments >$10,000 USD) requires filing a Currency Declaration Form (CDF) at Airport Customs.
	FCRA Alert: Public servants or politicians receiving foreign gifts must be flagged for FCRA compliance.
6. Immovable Property & Benami Laws
	Plain paper gift declarations are legally invalid for land, houses, or commercial property. Transfers strictly require a registered Gift Deed, payment of state Stamp Duty, and registration under the Registration Act.
	Funding a property or vehicle registered solely in a relative's or spouse's name without a documented gift or loan deed creates severe exposure under the Benami Transactions Prohibition Act.
7. Automatic Financial Reporting (SFT & AIS)
	High-value purchases or cash deposits trigger automatic reporting to the Income Tax Department via the Statement of Financial Transactions (SFT) and appear on the recipient's Annual Information Statement (AIS).
	Triggers include: Cash jewellery purchases >₹2,00,000, cash bank deposits >₹10,00,000, or property registrations >₹30,00,000. Alert users to maintain formal gift documentation to satisfy automated audit queries.
8. Special Asset Valuation (Rule 11UA)
	Gifts of unlisted company shares, Virtual Digital Assets (Crypto/NFTs), fine art, antiques, or bullion require a formal Valuation Report from a Registered Valuer based on Fair Market Value (FMV) as of the transfer date.
